Bursar

A design system for the calm, competent school administrator every proprietor wishes they had. Built for clarity around money and records, not for delight around features.

Design System · v1
Foundation

Why this system looks the way it does

The reference dashboards this was built from lean on bright gradient sidebars, rainbow donut charts and playful rounded icon tiles: the visual language of "exciting new app." Bursar's job is the opposite. Its audience has been burned by disorder before: a missing receipt booklet, a wrongly dialled number, a vendor who overcharged because there was no record to check. What reassures that person is not enthusiasm, it's evidence of order. So this system trades gradients for ruled lines, trades five-colour pie charts for two-colour comparisons that are readable at a glance, and treats every amount of money as something that must line up, literally, in a fixed-width column, so nothing can be misread.

Colour

Ledger, not gradient

Navy for authority and ink. Green for money that's accounted for. Brass for attention without alarm. Brick reserved only for things that are actually wrong. No colour appears purely for decoration.

Typography

One face for authority, one for reading, one for numbers

Manrope carries section titles and figures that need weight. Inter is the workhorse for every sentence a user reads. IBM Plex Mono is reserved entirely for money and dates, its fixed width means a ₦45,000 and a ₦4,500 can never be confused at a glance.

Signature element

The receipt card

Every confirmation in Bursar, a payment, a term summary, an onboarding milestone, renders as a torn-edge receipt. It's the one deliberately physical, tactile motif in an otherwise flat system, a direct callback to the paper booklets the product replaces.

What this system deliberately avoids

Instinct from the referencesBursar's version
Gradient sidebar, bright indigo/purpleSolid ink navy, one brass accent line marks the active item
Five-colour donut charts for income/expenseA sorted table or a two-colour comparison, green against slate
Emoji, exclamation points, "You're crushing it"Plain statements: amount, name, what's still safe
Soft rounded icon tiles in every card cornerIcons only where they aid scanning, never purely decorative
Rounded, bubbly corners everywhere (16 to 24px)Modest 8 to 10px radius, closer to a printed card than an app tile
