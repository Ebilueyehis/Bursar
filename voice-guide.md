# Product Voice Guide

## Naming: a quick suggestion before we start

The app would be called **Bursar**. 
A bursar is the real, respected person who handles a school's money and records. Your audience already knows and trusts the word. It is short, it works as a verb-adjacent brand ("get Bursar," "ask Bursar"), and it quietly reinforces the personality you already picked: a trusted school administrator. Other options if you want alternatives: **SchoolKeep**, **Ledger & Co**, **EduVault**. But Bursar is the strongest fit for the persona and the pain points in your founder story.

The rest of this guide uses **Bursar** as a placeholder name. Swap it out easily if you land on something else.

---

## 1. Who Bursar is

If Bursar were a person, they'd be the calm, competent school administrator every proprietor wishes they had from day one: someone who has seen every kind of chaos a school can produce and has a quiet, organized answer for all of it. Not a friend who commiserates. Not a salesperson who hypes. An administrator who has already done the work and is showing you where everything is.

**Three words:** Respectful. Clear. Confident.

- **Respectful** — never talks down to someone because they didn't grow up with software. Age and tech-comfort are not competence gaps.
- **Clear** — one meaning per sentence. No ambiguity around money, ever.
- **Confident** — states things plainly because it's sure of the numbers. Confidence here comes from accuracy, not enthusiasm.

Notably absent: playful, quirky, "reassuring" in the soft-comfort sense. This is a finance and records tool. The comfort it offers comes from precision and control, not warmth for its own sake.

---

## 2. The core tension the voice is solving

Your founder story is the brief. Every school owner using this product has, in some form, lived a version of: lost receipt booklets, no way to prove a debt, forgotten student details, wrong numbers dialed during registration, vendors quietly inflating invoices.

The underlying fear isn't "I don't understand technology." It's **"money or information could be slipping away and I won't know until it's too late."**

So the voice's real job is to be the opposite of that panic: exact, present, and unshakeable. Every screen should feel like it's saying, *this is accounted for.*

---

## 3. Sounds like / Doesn't sound like

| Situation | Sounds like Bursar | Doesn't sound like Bursar |
|---|---|---|
| Confirming a payment | "Payment of ₦45,000 received from Tunde Okafor's parent. Receipt #1082 generated." | "Yay! Cha-ching! Payment successful 🎉" |
| An error occurs | "This entry couldn't be saved. Your other records are safe. Please try again, or contact support if it happens again." | "Oops! Something went wrong on our end 😅" |
| Onboarding | "Let's add your first student. You'll need their name and class — we'll walk through the rest together." | "Ready to get started?! Let's gooo 🚀" |
| Explaining a feature | "This shows every outstanding balance, sorted from oldest to newest, so you always know who to follow up with first." | "Our smart dashboard leverages real-time sync to optimize your workflow." |
| A milestone (e.g. 100th student enrolled) | "You've now recorded 100 students in Bursar. That's 100 fewer names to remember on your own." | "WOOHOO! 100 students! You're crushing it!" |

Notice the celebratory moment still stays understated. Warmth here shows up as *acknowledging the relief*, not as exclamation points.

---

## 4. Vocabulary: keep it uniform everywhere

Inconsistent terms are one of the fastest ways to make an unfamiliar user feel lost, especially one who may lean on a child or grandchild to help. Pick one term per concept and never deviate, across app, emails, receipts, and support chat.

| Use this term | Not these |
|---|---|
| Receipt | Invoice, voucher, slip (pick receipt since it maps to what school owners already call it) |
| Outstanding balance | Debt, arrears, amount owed |
| Student record | Student profile, student file, student data |
| Guardian / Parent | User, contact, stakeholder |
| Payment received | Transaction successful, payment processed |
| Dashboard | Home, overview, control panel |

Build this into an actual glossary document as the product grows, and treat any new term as something that needs sign-off before it ships anywhere.

**On jargon generally:** avoid tech words like "sync," "cloud," or "dashboard" unless you've deliberately decided to teach them. If you do introduce a technical term, define it in plain language the first time it appears in any given flow: *"Your records are backed up automatically, so nothing is lost if your phone is lost or damaged."* Say the plain benefit, not the mechanism.

---

## 5. Moments that matter

**Onboarding — hand-holding, step by step**
Assume zero prior software experience. Never assume the user knows what to do next; tell them. One instruction per screen. Confirm before assuming ("We've added Tunde Okafor. Add another student, or move on to fees?").

**Errors — calm and apologetic**
Never blame the user. Always state what's still safe. Always give a next step.
> "This payment couldn't be recorded. No money has been deducted, and none of your existing records were affected. Please try again."

**Support / help text — expert guiding**
Bursar already knows the answer and is walking the user to it, not deflecting to a help center.
> "To edit a past receipt, go to the student's record and select 'Edit Payment.' You can't delete a receipt once issued, to keep your records accurate for auditing."

**Celebratory moments — earned, understated warmth**
These exist and they should feel good, but Bursar's version of celebration is relief and pride in order, not hype.
> "You've cleared every outstanding balance for this term. Everything is accounted for."

## 6. A first pass at brand story copy

For your about page or app intro, drawn from what you shared, tightened into brand voice:

> Bursar started with one school owner: my mother. In 2014, after twenty years of dreaming about it, she finally opened her own school. What she didn't expect was how much of running it would come down to paper she couldn't lose, numbers she had to remember, and people she had to chase down.
>
> I watched her panic over a missing receipt booklet, the only proof a family still owed fees. I watched her try to recall a child's details from memory because the paperwork had gone missing. I watched her dial the wrong number for a parent during registration, simply because she didn't have their surname on hand. And I watched vendors quietly charge her more than they should have, because there was no record to check against.
>
> None of that should still be a problem in the age of information. Bursar exists so no school owner has to hold all of that in their head, or lose sleep wondering if something slipped through. Every receipt, every student, every payment — accounted for, in one place.

This can be trimmed for different placements (a short version for the app's welcome screen, the fuller version for a website "About" page).

---

## 7. A working mission line

A few directions, all short enough to sit under a logo:

- **"Every naira accounted for."**
- **"Nothing lost. Nothing forgotten."**
- **"The school administrator every school owner deserves."**

I'd lean toward the first two — they speak directly to the founder story's core fear (money and information slipping away) without needing explanation.

---

## Where to apply this

Everywhere the product speaks: in-app copy, error states, onboarding, email and SMS notifications, receipts, support chat, the marketing site, and any printed material. Since your sales team will be handling in-person, local-language persuasion, this written voice only needs to hold in English for now, but keep the *principles* (respectful, clear, confident, no jargon without explanation) in mind if it's ever localized later.
